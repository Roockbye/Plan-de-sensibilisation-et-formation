## Why it matters

Phishing is the entry point for most cyberattacks. A single click on a rigged link can be enough to steal a password, install malware or trigger ransomware.

Attackers no longer stick to email:

- **by text message** (*smishing*): fake parcel, fake fine, fake refund;
- **by instant messaging** or social media;
- **by QR code** (*quishing*): the website address is not visible before scanning, which bypasses email filters;
- **with generative AI**: fraudulent messages are now flawless, personalised and written in perfect language. The absence of mistakes is **no longer** a sign of trust.

## Warning signs

| Sign | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Urgency or threats | “Your account will be deleted within 24 hours” |
| An unusual request | Entering your password, paying a fee, opening an unexpected attachment |
| An approximate sender | `support@{{organisation.domaine}}-securite.com` instead of `@{{organisation.domaine}}` |
| A link whose destination differs from its text | The text shows a known address, the link goes elsewhere |
| A generic greeting | “Dear user”, “Dear customer” |
| An offer that is too good | Unexpected prize, refund or gift |

> **Remember**: one sign is enough to have doubts. When in doubt, don't click, check through another channel and report it.

## Checking a link without clicking

1. **On a computer**: hover over the link with the mouse, or right-click and choose “Copy link address”, then read the full address.
2. **On a mobile phone**: press and hold the link to show the address without opening it.
3. **Read the domain from right to left** starting from the first `/`: in `https://{{organisation.domaine}}.connexion-verif.co/login`, the real domain is `connexion-verif.co`, not `{{organisation.domaine}}`.

## QR codes

A QR code is just a hidden link. Before scanning:

- ask yourself **who** put this QR code there (a sticker stuck on an official poster is suspicious);
- after scanning, **read the suggested address** before opening it;
- never enter your work credentials on a page opened from a QR code received by email.
